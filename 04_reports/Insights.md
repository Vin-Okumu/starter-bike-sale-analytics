# Insights and Recommendations

**Starter Bike LLC · Q1 2023 compared with Q1 2022**

All figures come from the Power BI dashboard with **Year = 2023** and no other filters. "Prior year" means the same months in 2022 (January to March). Revenue excludes cancelled order lines and tax.

| Business question | Insight | Recommended action |

## 1. Executive Overview: are we growing, and what's driving it?

*For the owner / GM*

| KPI | Q1 2023 | Q1 2022 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $337.6K | $247.3K | ▲ 36.5% |
| Gross profit | $103.2K | $76.1K | ▲ 35.5% |
| Gross margin | 30.6% | 30.8% | ≈ flat |
| Bikes sold | 178 | 127 | ▲ 40.2% |
| Order value lost to cancellations | $97.5K | $69.3K | ▲ 40.7% |

- **Growth was broad, not a one-off month.** Revenue beat Q1 2022 in January ($111.2K), February ($105.9K) and March ($120.5K, the best month).
- **One line carries the business.** Mountain Bikes earned $164.2K, 49% of revenue. Touring Bikes ($84.8K) are a distant second, and the two together make up 74%.
- **Margin held while volume grew.** Gross margin stayed at about 31%, so the extra revenue came from selling more, not from discounting.
- **Cancellations grew faster than sales.** Lost order value rose 40.7%, slightly ahead of revenue, and 99% of it came from Road Bikes ($96.3K).

## 2. Products & Margins: which product lines should we push, protect or cut?

*For the owner / buyer*

- **The four big lines all sit at 29–32% margin.** Mountain 29.1%, Touring 31.8%, Road 30.7% and E-Bikes 31.9%. No large line is unusually profitable or unprofitable.
- **The small lines earn more per sale.** Hybrid, BMX and Kids Bikes earn 40% margins but bring in only 2.6% of revenue ($8.9K together). They were also new in 2023: the business sold 7 categories, up from 4 in Q1 2022.
- **Top model:** EnduroMaster 1000 brought in $27.6K, up 50% from $18.4K. The top 10 models are led by Mountain models (EnduroMaster, GravityMaster).
- **PayPal buyers spend more per line.** A PayPal order line averages $3,227, against $2,287 for credit card (41% larger). Card is used on 56% of fulfilled lines, yet PayPal brings in more revenue ($177.5K vs $160.1K).

## 3. Orders & Cancellations: where are orders failing, and why?

*For the operations manager*

| Order lines | Q1 2023 | Q1 2022 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Shipped | 50.3% | 50.0% |
| Still processing | 37.1% | 37.3% |
| Cancelled | 12.6% | 12.7% |

- **The problem is concentrated in one line.** 17 of 37 Road Bike order lines were cancelled (46%). Among the larger categories, Road Bikes is the only one with any cancellations; the only other one was a single BMX line ($1.2K).
- **It is recurring, not a blip.** The cancellation rate held near 13% in both years (11.1%, 13.0% and 13.5% by month in 2023). In Q1 2022 Road Bikes were again the only category with cancellations ($69.3K, 50% of their lines).
- **Five models account for every Road Bike cancellation:**

| Model | Lines cancelled | Order value lost | Share of loss |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Voyager 1000 | 6 | $30.6K | 31.4% |
| SpeedMaster 1000 | 4 | $21.6K | 22.2% |
| CrossRider 1000 | 3 | $17.1K | 17.5% |
| AeroSpeed 1000 | 2 | $16.8K | 17.2% |
| GravelMaster 1000 | 2 | $10.2K | 10.5% |
| FreestyleMaster 1000 (BMX) | 1 | $1.2K | 1.2% |
| **Total** | **18** | **$97.5K** | **100%** |

- **A large share is still in progress.** At report date, $131.4K (30%) of Q1 2023 order value was still processing. $206.2K (47%) had shipped and $97.5K (22%) was cancelled.

## 4. Tax: how much do we owe, and where does it come from?

*For the bookkeeper*

- **Tax due is $13,915**, up 33.3% from $10,435, in line with revenue growth. It was higher than the prior year in every month: January $4,635, February $4,415, March $4,865 (the highest).
- **Effective rate: about 4.1% of revenue** (4.17%, 4.17% and 4.04% by month).
- **Who triggers it.** 87 of 125 fulfilled lines (69.6%) were over the $2,000 threshold, down from 74.2%. The new, cheaper lines (BMX and Kids) never reach it.
- **Where it comes from.** Mountain Bikes generate 52% of tax due ($7,170). Then Touring $3,120, Road $1,725, E-Bikes $1,660 and Hybrid $240.
- **How it works.** Tax is 5% on each fulfilled order line over $2,000 and is added to the customer's bill: $337.6K revenue + $13.9K tax = $351.5K billed. The business collects it and remits it, so it does not reduce revenue or profit.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Fix Road Bike cancellations.** Check stock availability, delivery lead times and pricing on the five models above, starting with Voyager 1000. Recovering half the lost value would add about $48K a quarter.
2. **Protect the Mountain Bike line.** It carries 49% of revenue and 52% of tax. Prioritise its stock levels and supplier terms before next Q1.
3. **Lean into PayPal.** Feature it at checkout and target promotions at PayPal customers to raise the average order line value.
4. **Decide on the small lines.** Hybrid, BMX and Kids Bikes have the best margins but only a sliver of revenue. Test whether they can grow before cutting them.
5. **Clear the processing backlog.** Track the $131.4K still processing monthly, so it ships rather than turning into more cancellations.
6. **Set aside tax as you go.** Reserve about 4% of revenue each month for remittance.

## Limits of this analysis

- **Two first quarters only.** The data covers January to March of 2022 and 2023, so it shows year-on-year change, not seasonality across a full year.
- **No reason for cancellation is recorded.** The data shows where cancellations happen, not why. The recommendations on Road Bikes are where to look, not a diagnosis.
- **Order and Customer IDs don't identify single orders.** Each Order ID maps to one Customer ID and covers several lines across different dates and both years, so the analysis works in order lines and doesn't cover repeat purchases or basket size.
- **Three flagged data issues** (one line priced below wholesale, two possible duplicate lines) are kept as recorded and affect under 2% of revenue; see the [data audit](../01_docs/Data_audit_report.md).
- **Fictional company.** Starter Bike LLC is a fictional retailer built for this portfolio project.
